How this board works
  • What you see is derived, not typed. Each entry is stored once in a data file. Whether it shows as open, closing soon, applied or closed is worked out from its deadline and today's date, so a deadline passing moves it without anyone editing it.
  • Open means a specific call you can apply to now. Groups, programmes and contacts with no call open are leads. Closed entries stay searchable under Leads & resources.
  • Scores are human. Fit scores come from the weekly review against the rubric below. Nothing automated scores or adds an opening.
  • Links are checked by machine. Twice a week a job on GitHub's servers requests every tracked link and records whether it still leads to a real page. A problem shows on the entry itself.
  • Discover is unreviewed. The same job reads job-board feeds and lists postings that match your keywords. They are pointers to check, not recommendations.

Habits that land these posts
  1. Email the PI before you apply. The strongest candidates arrive already known — a short, specific note about the group's last two papers beats a cold application.
  2. Bring your own money. A portable fellowship (MSCA-PF, Humboldt) makes you cheap to host and lets you pick the lab — fellowship beats project on ties.
  3. Submit 3–4 days early, never on deadline day. Portals fail; referees go quiet. Slack in the schedule is a feature, not laziness.
  4. Tailor one CV per region. Photo for Japan, none for Europe; lead with the one verified, quantified result.
  5. Line up referees in writing first. A silent referee sinks an otherwise strong file.
  6. Make every application serve one story — the India-return arc — so reviewers see a trajectory, not scattershot.
