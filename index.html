🎯 How this fits Rona

This board is tuned to one profile, not a generic search. Every opening is scored on what actually decides Rona's move: family finances and spouse work rights (30%), how well a group's ISV/BSISO + radar + ML work matches her toolkit (25%), and how much the post's brand helps a later return to an Indian scientist role (20%). Her signature result — a GRU-LSTM system that called the 2026 monsoon onset in real time, verified within a day of the IMD date — is a day-one asset for QPE, nowcasting, and intraseasonal-prediction groups, which is why radar/ML posts rank highest. Proof points on file: URSI AP-RASC 2025 Top-3 Student Paper Prize, CARE-25 Best Paper, an AGU 2024 travel grant, the DST-INSPIRE fellowship, and hands-on work on the world's first 205 MHz VHF ST wind-profiling radar.

Top-3% habits that actually land these posts
  1. Email the PI before you apply. The strongest candidates arrive already known — a short, specific note about the group's last two papers beats a cold application.
  2. Bring your own money. A portable fellowship (MSCA-PF, Humboldt) makes you cheap to host and lets you pick the lab — fellowship beats project on ties.
  3. Submit 3–4 days early, never on deadline day. Portals fail; referees go quiet. Slack in the schedule is a feature, not laziness.
  4. Tailor one CV per region. Photo for Japan, none for Europe; lead with the one verified, quantified result, not a wall of publications.
  5. Line up referees in writing first. Confirm each referee before you list them — a silent referee sinks an otherwise strong file.
  6. Make every application serve one story. Here, the India-return arc — so reviewers see a trajectory, not a scattershot.
